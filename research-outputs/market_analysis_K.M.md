## Part 1: Competitive landscape

**The global supply picture.** The United States register lists 88 oncology injectable package presentations across
12 medicines and 20 companies. Of the 59 packages with a current status, 28 are unavailable, 11 are of limited
availability and 20 are available. The remaining 29 rows are marked to be discontinued and carry no availability
value at all.

Carboplatin dominates the register: 35 package rows from 9 companies, of which 20 were unavailable on 7 August 2026.
By 15 October the unavailable count had fallen to 16, while all 35 original rows remained listed. Ifosfamide had 7
rows from 3 companies, with 6 unavailable in August falling to 3 by October; a new package from SteriMax appeared
after August, bringing the October list to 8 packages from 4 companies. Methotrexate had 14 rows from 5 companies,
none unavailable in August, but 4 unavailable by 15 October.

**The South African service landscape.** ICON Oncology operates 66 chemotherapy facilities, employs 161 oncologists
and holds contracts with 32 or more medical schemes, and it runs its own formulary. Medipost runs a dedicated
oncology courier pharmacy facility. No dedicated oncology unit could be found at Clicks in the published record.
Discovery Health introduced reference pricing for cancer generics in January 2025, and its average cost per cancer
claimant reached R143,000 in 2024.

**Import dependence.** About 65 per cent of medicines used in South Africa are imported and about 98 per cent of
active pharmaceutical ingredients are imported. In parliamentary question NW2997, dated 26 June 2026, the Minister
of Health said that oncology supply remains challenging because of reliance on a limited number of international
manufacturers.

**Pricing structure.** The Single Exit Price is a regulated maximum and buying below it is prohibited, so holding
deeper stock earns no advantage on the purchase price. The dispensing fee above R1,530 is R270.54 plus 5 per cent.
The incentive to hold buffer stock is therefore continuity of treatment, not purchasing gain.

## Part 2: Key trends and signals

**Trend 1. Persistent shortages concentrated on a few molecules.** Ifosfamide is the only molecule flagged as an
active shortage by all four regulators at once: the United States (7 of 7 rows current, 6 unavailable in August),
the European Medicines Agency (active since 12 February 2026, last updated 3 March 2026), the French agency
(Holoxan 1 g and 2 g supply tension since 13 January 2026) and the Italian agency (1 shortage row started by 20
October 2026). Carboplatin is listed by two regulators, the United States and Italy, where there are 11 shortage
rows across 5 licence holders. Methotrexate is listed by three: the United States, France (an Accord stock-out
running since 12 January 2023) and Italy (2 rows). The European entry for methotrexate is resolved.

**Trend 2. Manufacturer exits are thinning the supplier base.** Of the 88 rows, 29 are marked to be discontinued.
**Nine of the twelve molecules** have at least some packages marked for discontinuation: carboplatin, oxaliplatin,
irinotecan, gemcitabine, docetaxel, bendamustine, pemetrexed, decitabine and arsenic trioxide. Separately, and not
to be confused with that, the stated reasons across the 88 rows include discontinuation of manufacture on 7 rows,
"other" on 16 and demand increase on 9. When a manufacturer leaves, the remaining suppliers carry higher demand on a
smaller base.

**Trend 3. The South African disruptions only partly overlap the global picture.** The parliamentary reply names
four oncology products: bleomycin, carboplatin 450 mg per 45 ml (manufacturing delays, alternative supplier),
docetaxel 20 mg per ml (manufacturing constraints, paclitaxel substituted) and filgrastim. Carboplatin appears on
both the South African list and the overseas registers. Docetaxel appears on the United States register (2 rows,
both to be discontinued, from 1 company) and in Italy (4 rows from 2 licence holders), and in the South African
reply. But bleomycin, filgrastim and paclitaxel do not appear in the twelve-row summary at all. The two records
therefore only partly overlap and must not be treated as confirming each other where they diverge.

**Trend 4. Funder pressure on generic oncology pricing.** Reference pricing from January 2025 pushes down what a
pharmacy can recover, and the ban on buying below the Single Exit Price removes the other side of the margin. The
money available to fund buffer stock is squeezed from both directions.

## Part 3: Opportunities and risks

**Risks**

- **Carboplatin fragility.** Twenty of 35 United States packages were unavailable on 7 August 2026, improving only
  to 16 by 15 October, with 11 Italian rows across 5 licence holders, and South Africa has already had a carboplatin
  disruption. The combination of high overseas unavailability and a confirmed local disruption makes it the
  highest-risk molecule in the set.
- **Ifosfamide global constraint.** Four regulators flag it at once. It is low volume, at 1,554 United States
  Medicaid prescriptions in 2025, but four-regulator convergence means alternative sourcing from any major market is
  unlikely to be straightforward.
- **Methotrexate deterioration.** None unavailable in August, four by October, with a French stock-out running since
  January 2023. The direction of travel warrants monitoring even though it is not named in the South African reply.
- **No national stock audit.** The Minister confirmed no national stock audit in the previous twelve months, and
  South Africa publishes no shortage register, so the four-product list may understate the true position.
- **Margin constraint on buffer stock.** Holding extra weeks of cover ties up working capital with no offsetting
  benefit on the purchase price.

**Opportunities**

- **Formulary-led resilience.** A network with its own formulary and 66 facilities has the scale to negotiate with
  manufacturers and move stock between sites. A pharmacy that can demonstrate reliable supply strengthens its case
  in scheme contracts.
- **Cold-chain courier capability.** A dedicated oncology courier facility already exists in this market, which
  shows the logistics model is viable and can reduce wastage through better stock rotation.
- **Monitoring the overseas registers is a hypothesis worth testing, not a proven early warning.** It is plausible
  that overseas deterioration precedes a South African disruption, given the import dependence. But only two
  molecules appear on both records, which is too few to establish a pattern; there is no South African register
  against which timing could be compared; and the strongest overseas signal of all, ifosfamide at four regulators,
  was never named in the South African reply, which is a direct counter-example. The way to test it is to compare
  overseas register movements against wholesaler back-order data over time.
- **Reference pricing as a differentiator.** Reference pricing rewards a pharmacy that can reliably supply the
  reference generic rather than being forced to a costlier alternative, which protects both the fee and the patient
  from a co-payment.

## Part 4: Evidence note

The analysis uses exactly three sources, and every figure in it carries one of three labels.

1. **The United States register**, a snapshot of the United States medicines regulator's shortage database taken on
   7 August 2026. The oncology injectable working set is 88 package rows over 12 medicines and 20 companies.
   Availability is recorded only for the 59 current rows; the 29 rows marked to be discontinued leave it blank.
2. **The twelve-row summary**, one row per molecule, recording what four regulators report. Its October United
   States column reflects 15 October 2026; the Italian figures were listed as at 20 October 2026; the European entry
   for ifosfamide was last updated 3 March 2026 and the French one on 24 July 2026.
3. **The external research**, published South African sources, including parliamentary question NW2997 of 26 June
   2026, the import dependence figures, the Single Exit Price and dispensing fee rules, and public information on
   ICON Oncology, Discovery Health, Clicks and Medipost.

**What the sources do not contain.** Neither data file holds South African data, South African regulator data,
wholesaler inventory or any price. No South African shortage beyond the four named products has been stated or
implied. The United States register is a rolling twelve-month window, so a listing that has disappeared, as
gemcitabine and pemetrexed had by 15 October 2026, has not necessarily been resolved.

## The two corrections made before this analysis was accepted

Both were found by recomputing the agent's figures from the raw files, not by reading its prose.

1. **A counting error.** The first draft said that seven of the twelve molecules had packages marked for
   discontinuation. The true figure is nine. The number seven belongs to something else entirely: the seven rows
   whose stated reason is discontinuation of manufacture. The reissued document states both figures separately and
   says explicitly that they are different things.
2. **An overreach.** The first draft claimed that deterioration on the overseas registers "often precedes a South
   African disruption" and could give "weeks or months of advance warning", citing carboplatin and docetaxel. That
   is not supported by anything in the evidence: two cases are not a pattern, there is no South African register in
   which the timing could be observed, and ifosfamide is a direct counter-example. It was replaced with the honest
   version now in Part 3.

The second correction matters more than the first. A wrong count is visible to anyone who recomputes it. A
plausible-sounding causal claim is not, and it would have become the recommendation of the brief.
